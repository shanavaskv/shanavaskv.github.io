The discovery of a superconducting transition temperature of $T_c = 190$ K in metallic H₂S under pressures of 200 GPa renewed interest in the superconducting properties of metal-hydrogen systems. These materials are expected to be electron-phonon superconductors, and hydrogen, with its low mass, contributes new optic phonons that may couple with the conduction electrons. Often, though not always, a large electron-phonon coupling parameter $\lambda$ (and consequently a high $T_c$) results from a high electronic density of states at the Fermi level, $N(E_F)$, and the presence of soft phonons.

Our first-principles calculations of cubic TiH₂ show a large 3d density of states, $N(E_F) = 2.8$ states/eV/f.u. The calculated phonon dispersions show Ti modes active below 10 THz, whereas the much lighter H modes are active between 32 and 40 THz. Electron-phonon coupling calculations give $\lambda = 0.98$, which corresponds to $T_c = 6.1$ K. However, the large $N(E_F)$ also leads to a tetragonal instability at low temperatures, which may be overcome by uniaxial strain, potentially making TiH₂ a candidate electron-phonon superconductor.
